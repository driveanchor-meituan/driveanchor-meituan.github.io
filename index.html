DriveAnchor generates route- and maneuver-compliant trajectories from a shared anchor vocabulary and runs on a real vehicle in urban traffic.

Abstract

Autonomous-driving planners must generate route- and maneuver-compliant trajectories under a limited decoder-call budget while adapting offline to black-box static-collision feedback. We propose DriveAnchor, a three-stage framework built around a shared vocabulary of recorded trajectories and a continuous trajectory interface. The vocabulary supports flow-matching (FM) residual learning, Energy Field (EF) corridor-conditioned initialization, and anchor-guided zeroth-order reward fine-tuning. The reward update operates in trajectory space without policy likelihoods or reward gradients, enabling offline adaptation of a deterministic decoder.

On sampled subsets of nuPlan Val14 and Test14-hard per simulation mode, DriveAnchor achieves the highest reactive scores among the compared methods: 81.50 and 65.74, respectively. We further integrate the trajectory generator into a real vehicle's planning pipeline and demonstrate its operation on urban roads. On 10,000 held-out internal scenarios, under the original non-EF prior and a fixed two-call FM budget, reward fine-tuning reduces short- and long-horizon static candidate collision rates from 27.22% and 56.54% to 2.94% and 7.15%, respectively, and improves 8-s best-of-set minADE from 2.27 to 1.34 m. In a separate junction diagnostic on checkpoints before Stage 3, one and two EF calls raise corridor-guidance satisfaction from 22.10% to 74.13% and 87.92%, respectively. Additional ablations support the benefits of real-trajectory priors and anchor-guided exploration. In real-vehicle tests, trajectory generation takes 2.06 ms on average.

Method

Figure 1: Overview of DriveAnchor. A shared trajectory vocabulary connects FM residual learning, EF request control, and offline anchor-guided zeroth-order reward adaptation. At deployment the scene is encoded once, EF adjusts the initialization, and FM refines it for the downstream selector.

Shared trajectory vocabulary

Recorded trajectories provide structured starting points for supervised correction and output-space exploration, preserving multimodality when the deterministic decoder produces similar candidates.

Progressive training

Stage 1 learns FM residuals from local anchor–ground-truth pairs. Stage 2 learns corridor-conditioned EF initialization jointly with FM. Stage 3 adapts FM with black-box reward feedback while retaining regression.

Likelihood-free adaptation

Vocabulary neighbors define exploration directions. Reward differences provide a zeroth-order signal in trajectory space, without policy likelihoods or reward gradients.

🌟 41-minute urban drive

This recording covers one on-road test session in urban traffic. DriveAnchor generated the executed trajectories continuously for the whole 41-minute route, and the safety driver did not take over at any point.

The route covers a wide range of scenarios: intersections, merging and weaving areas, and lane changes, including short-window lane changes and consecutive multi-lane changes. Along the way the vehicle negotiates with surrounding motor vehicles and yields to crossing pedestrians and non-motorized road users. The eight clips above are excerpts from this recording.
